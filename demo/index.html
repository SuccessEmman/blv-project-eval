Evaluating AI-generated image captions for blind and low-vision users

A caption is only useful if you can trust it.

Blind and low-vision people ask AI to describe photos they can't see, and they usually can't check the answer. We tested whether two captioning models stay honest when a photo is dark or blurry, and whether they read the text that matters.

Findings

Four reviewers scored all 500 captions for Error Honesty and Text Recognition. We then compared them with an AI judge and with CIDEr, the standard caption metric.

  • κ 0.63 honesty 0.85 text

    Our reviewers agree

    Average Cohen's kappa across the six reviewer pairs. Both meet our target of 0.6.

  • ρ 0.63 honesty 0.71 text

    An AI judge mostly agrees with people

    Spearman's rho between Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite and our reviewers. Text meets our 0.65 target; honesty falls just short. The judge was far kinder to BLIP-2's short captions (mean 1.16) than people were (0.84).

  • 80.8 BLIP-2 0.1 LLaVA

    CIDEr exaggerates, and misses text

    CIDEr (×100) rates BLIP-2 far above LLaVA-1.5, higher on 211 of 217 photos, largely a penalty on LLaVA's long captions. Our reviewers see a small honesty gap (0.84 vs 0.74) and no text gap (0.85 vs 0.85). CIDEr barely follows honesty (Kendall's τ 0.21) and not text reading (τ 0.08).

  • 19 of 102

    Most captions miss the text

    On photos with agreed key text, 36 captions ignored it, 39 read part of it, 8 misread it and only 19 read it fully.

Our hypothesis, tested

We predicted that under degraded conditions the model CIDEr ranks higher would not be more honest or read text better. Our hypothesis was supported. CIDEr puts BLIP-2 far ahead, but on blurry and low-light photos BLIP-2 was no more honest than LLaVA-1.5, and on text the two models were tied (0.85 each). BLIP-2's small overall honesty lead comes from clear and text photos, and CIDEr turns that small gap into a near-total one. Both models missed the key text in most photos, as we predicted.

Method

  1. Sample

    250 VizWiz photos taken by blind photographers: 70 clear, 70 blurry, 60 low light, 50 with text.

  2. Caption

    BLIP-2 and LLaVA-1.5 each describe every photo with the same prompt and fixed settings, giving 500 captions.

  3. Score

    Four reviewers score every caption on their own, without knowing which model wrote it, followed by a review pass.

  4. Judge

    Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite scores the same captions with the same rubric and reference text.

  5. Compare

    Reviewer agreement (Cohen's κ), judge versus people (Spearman's ρ) and CIDEr versus people (Kendall's τ).
